Weekly schedule maker: where your 168 hours go

A week has 168 hours. Put in the blocks that are not negotiable (sleep, work, commute, meals, the school run) with their days and times, and the page draws the week, totals each block, and shows what is really left: free hours per day and, more useful, the longest free stretch of each day, which is where a workout or an hour of focused work can fit. Blocks that overlap are flagged with the minutes they share.

How the hours are counted

Every block runs from its start time to its end time on each day ticked. When the end is earlier than the start (sleep from 23:00 to 07:00) the block runs past midnight into the next day, and Sunday night runs into Monday morning. Same start and end means 24 hours. Totals per block count each of its minutes, so two blocks that overlap are both counted in their own total, while the busy and free hours of a day count a minute once, whichever block it belongs to. That is why the clash list matters: it shows the minutes counted twice.

Why the longest free stretch

Four free hours made of eight half-hours do not hold a gym session or a deep work block. The longest free stretch is the longest run of minutes with no block, inside the day from midnight to midnight. If it is under an hour on most weekdays, the fix is to move one block, not to find more time.

Limits

Fixed blocks only: the page does not schedule tasks for you. Times go in 15-minute steps or finer, there is no time zone handling, and nothing is sent anywhere: the week is stored in this browser only.

Keep it on paper

Once the fixed blocks are known, the week itself (appointments by day, three goals, quick and deep tasks, a Sunday review that carries over) fits on a printable weekly planner, one spread per week.
